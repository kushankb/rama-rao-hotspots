Method. Indicators follow the Atlas definitions in each model: unusually hot (Mar–May) and cold (Dec–Feb) days at ±4 °C from the 1985–2014 normal for that date; drought proneness as 2 × severe (>50% annual rainfall deficit) + moderate (26–50%) years; dry-spell score for Jun–Oct spells of ≥14 days below 2.5 mm. Changes are district means of the 0.1° grid. Periods: ~2047 = 2033–2062, ~2050 = 2036–2065, ~2100 = 2071–2100.

Rama Rao index: change truncated at the 1st and 99th percentile, normalised as ±X/(max−min) or ±X/|X|max, weighted by Atlas Table 5c, classed by ±0.5 and ±1.5 SD around zero, computed per model; the median model value and the number of models agreeing are shown. Thresholds view: rainfall or rainy days −10%, Tmax or Tmin +1 °C, hot days +5, cold days +2, frost +5 days, drought +5 points, dry-spell score +1, 99th-percentile rain +5 mm, 100 mm events +0.5 a year, max 1- or 3-day rain share +10% relative; more rain is neutral where baseline rainfall is at least 1,500 mm. Ensemble mean: the earlier INDRA-CMIP6 14-model average, where hot and cold days use ±2 SD and drought uses SPI-3 because averaging removes day-to-day variability.

Source: Rama Rao C.A. et al. (2020), Atlas on Risk and Vulnerability of Indian Agriculture to Climate Change, ICAR-CRIDA. Part of FAO TCP/IND/4005.
